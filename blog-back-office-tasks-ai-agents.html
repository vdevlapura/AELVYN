Five back-office tasks a UAE business can hand to an agent

Aelvyn, Dubai, 19 Oct 2026

Five pieces of weekly admin an agent can prepare for a business of 5 to 200 people, with what it reads, what it drafts and what it waits for, task by task.

Walk into the back office of a trading company in Al Quoz or a clinic in Jumeirah on a Thursday afternoon and the work looks much the same. Someone is retyping a supplier invoice into the accounting system. Someone is working out which customer the transfer on the bank statement came from. Someone is answering the same delivery question on WhatsApp again. None of this is the business. All of it has to be done, usually by the two or three people who also run the business.

This note lists five of those tasks, the ones we expect a business of 5 to 200 people in the UAE to hand over first. For each one it sets out what an agent reads, what it prepares and what it waits for. The pattern is the same throughout: the agent does the reading and the drafting, and a person does the signing.

01Supplier invoices, from inbox to draft bill

A supplier invoice in the UAE carries a 15-digit TRN, a 5% VAT line, a due date and often an Arabic name beside the English one. It arrives as a PDF attached to an email, or as a photo from a phone. Someone opens it, finds the purchase order, checks quantities and unit prices, and types the lot into the accounting system. Then the owner or finance lead checks it again before paying.

An agent can do everything in that paragraph except the check and the payment. It reads the document as it lands and extracts the fields. It finds the purchase order and compares the two, line by line. Where they disagree, it says so. Then it prepares a draft bill and stops.

Reads
Invoices and receipts from the accounts inbox, PDF or photo, English or Arabic. The matching purchase order.
Prepares
The invoice as a record: supplier, TRN, number, dates, lines, VAT, total. A line-by-line match, differences flagged. A draft bill, not posted.
Waits for
A person to approve the draft before it is posted, and to schedule any payment.

The agent reads and drafts. A person signs. That order never changes.

02Payments, in and out

Money moves in two directions and both need matching. A customer pays by transfer and the statement line shows only a reference and an amount. Someone has to work out which invoice it settles. Other customers have not paid at all, and someone has to notice, draft a firm but polite reminder, and send it in the customer's language.

An agent can read payments as they are recorded in the accounting system and set them against open invoices. It proposes which payment settles which invoice and leaves the match for a person to confirm. It lists what is overdue, drafts the reminder, and does not send it. A reminder that reaches a customer whose cheque is already in your drawer costs more goodwill than the invoice was worth, and the agent cannot see your drawer.

Reads
Payments recorded in the accounting system. Open customer invoices and their due dates.
Prepares
Which payment settles which invoice. Overdue invoices by age. A reminder in English or Arabic, ready to send.
Waits for
A person to confirm each match and to send every reminder.

03Leads and follow-ups

A new enquiry arrives on WhatsApp at nine in the evening. Another comes through the website form. A third is an email forwarded from a colleague. By Monday morning two of the three are in the CRM, one with a digit missing from the phone number, and the promised follow-up has not gone out. It is what happens when the same information has to be typed twice.

An agent can capture the enquiry where it lands, create or update the CRM record, and draft the first reply. The draft waits for a person to read and send. Nothing reaches the lead that a person has not seen.

Reads
New enquiries from email, web forms and WhatsApp. Any existing CRM record.
Prepares
A new or updated CRM record: contact, source, what was asked. A follow-up drafted in the lead's language.
Waits for
A person to approve the CRM change, by default, and to send any message to the lead.

04Customer questions, in two languages

Every business has a short list of questions it answers all day. What time do you open. Is my order on its way. Do you deliver to Sharjah. The answers are already written down, in a delivery policy, a price list or a booking page. The problem is the volume, and the fact that the question arrives in Arabic as often as in English.

An agent that has read your own documents can answer in the language the customer wrote in. The important word is "your". It does not answer from general knowledge. If your delivery policy does not say whether you deliver on a Friday, the agent does not guess. It hands the conversation to your team with a summary of what was asked and what has been said.

Reads
The customer's message, in English or Arabic. Your own documents, and nothing else.
Prepares
A reply in the customer's language, naming the source document. Otherwise, a summary for your team.
Waits for
Refunds, exceptions and anything the documents do not cover. You decide whether routine replies go out alone.

05The daily and weekly numbers

Most owners ask for the same numbers every week. What did we sell. Who owes us money, and for how long. What did we spend with each supplier. Today someone exports from the accounting system, someone else from the CRM, and a third person pastes both into a spreadsheet and writes two lines of commentary.

An agent can read across those systems and write the summary in plain language every morning and every week. It can also answer a direct question in plain words: who owes us the most, what did we spend with Al Noor this quarter. This is the one task on the list where the agent waits for nothing, because it only reads and never changes a record. A wrong summary is corrected by the next one. A wrong bill is a different matter, which is why the other four tasks wait.

Reads
The accounting system, the CRM and the spreadsheets you already keep.
Prepares
A plain-language summary each morning and each week. Answers to direct questions, with the figures.
Waits for
Nothing. It only reads.
